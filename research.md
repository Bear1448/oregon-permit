## 1. Permit eligibility (Provisional Instruction Permit, under 18)

Oregon calls a Class C instruction permit issued to drivers 15, 16, or 17 a **Provisional Instruction Permit**. ([Under-18 permit page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/permit.aspx))

### Minimum age

- At least **15 years old** to apply for an instruction permit. ([Driver Manual p. 89](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf); [Driver Information](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_quick_tips/driver_information.aspx); [Under-18 permit page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/permit.aspx))
- Applicant must be an **Oregon resident**. ([Driver Information](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_quick_tips/driver_information.aspx))

### What she must bring / ID and residency

To be **issued** the permit she must go to a DMV office (even if the knowledge test was passed online) and:

- Present **proof of identity and date of birth**. Documents must be **original or certified copies** from the issuing agency; photocopies are not accepted. ([Manual pp. 90–91](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf); [Form 7318](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/Forms/DMV/7318.pdf))
- Present **proof of current residence address** (one document for a standard, non-REAL ID card; **two** documents from two different sources for REAL ID). ([Manual p. 91](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf); [Driver Information](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_quick_tips/driver_information.aspx))
- Provide a **Social Security number**, or electronically certify that she has not been assigned one. She is not required to bring the Social Security **card**. ([Under-18 permit page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/permit.aspx); [Online Knowledge Testing FAQ](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_services/online_knowledge_testing.aspx))
- Pay the issuance fee and have her photo taken. Hats, eyeglasses, face paint, or contacts that change the appearance of the eye are not allowed in the photo. ([Under-18 permit page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/permit.aspx); [Manual p. 89](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf))

**Most common identity documents** (from the manual’s “most common forms” list, pp. 90–91): U.S. government-issued **birth certificate** (hospital-issued certificates and baptismal records are **not** acceptable); U.S. passport or passport card valid or expired no more than 5 years; Oregon tribal ID approved by DMV; Certificate of Naturalization or Citizenship; and other listed primary documents. A complete list is in [Form 7318](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/Forms/DMV/7318.pdf) and [this DMV identity/address PDF](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/DMV/docs/Proof_Identity_DOB_Full_Legal_Name_ResAddress.pdf). Rule of thumb from Form 7318: **one primary** identity document, **or two secondary** documents, **or one secondary plus a DMV photo on file less than 13 years old**.

**Common proof of address** (manual p. 91): mail from a business, school, or government agency with first and last name (personal mail and forwarded mail are not acceptable); a document from an educational provider; a utility hookup order, bill, mortgage document, or property tax statement; **or** certification by a person who lives at the same address, if that person accompanies the applicant and shows their own address proof (for example their license or ID card).

**REAL ID is optional** for the permit. It costs an extra $30 on top of the regular issuance fee and requires additional documents, including proof of lawful U.S. status and two address proofs. ([Driver Information](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_quick_tips/driver_information.aspx); [Oregon.gov/REALID](https://www.oregon.gov/REALID)) A teen under 18 does **not** need REAL ID to fly; the federal REAL ID air-travel rule described in the manual applies to people **18 and older**. ([Manual, inside front cover](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf))

**Every time she takes a test,** official sources agree she must pay the test fee and show proof of identity and date of birth. The 2026–2027 printed manual (p. 1) also says to present **current residence address** every time she tests; the online Testing chapter says identity and date of birth (and, for issuance, residence address). Use the printed manual plus the office’s instructions on test day. ([Manual p. 1](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf); [Online Testing chapter](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/DMV/Pages/Online_Manual/Testing.aspx); [Driver Information](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_quick_tips/driver_information.aspx))

### Parental consent and school enrollment

If she is under 18 and not married or emancipated:

- A **parent or legal guardian must sign** the application. The signature is captured electronically at a field office. If the parent cannot come in, they sign [Parent/Guardian Certification Form 735-173DP](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/Forms/DMV/173DPfill.pdf). ([Consent page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/consent.aspx); [Manual p. 89](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf))
- For a **first-time** Oregon driving privilege, a parent or legal guardian must **certify she is enrolled in or attending school**. If she is no longer in school, she must present a high school diploma or GED certificate. ([Manual p. 89](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf); Form 735-173DP cites [ORS 807.066](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors807.html))
- ORS 807.066 also allows the parent to certify enrollment in a school in another state or country, community-college progress toward a diploma/GED, homeschooling under ORS 339.035, or a listed attendance exemption. ([ORS 807.066](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors807.html))
- The person who signed can **cancel** the privilege until she turns 18, using the Request to Cancel Driving Privileges by Parent or Legal Guardian form. ([Consent page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/consent.aspx))

Married or emancipated minors do not need parental consent but must show documentation. ([Consent page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/consent.aspx))

### Vision screening

- Given at a DMV office when she applies for the permit. There is **no fee**. If she takes it wearing glasses or contacts, her privilege will require corrective lenses. If she does not pass, DMV gives her a form to take to a licensed vision specialist (optometrist or ophthalmologist). ([Manual p. 1](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf); [Under-18 permit page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/permit.aspx))
- Official **state standard** on [Certificate of Vision Form 735-24 (10-26)](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/24fill.pdf), citing OAR 735-062-0050 and ORS 807.090: unrestricted driving is a **110-degree visual field** and **20/40 or better acuity** (without correction for an unrestricted card; with correction for a “B — corrective lenses” restriction). Worse than 20/40 but at least 20/70 may qualify only with restrictions (for example daylight-only). I did **not** find a public official description of the exact chart or procedure used on the office screening machine itself—only that DMV tests eyesight “to confirm you can see well enough to drive safely,” and refers failures to a specialist using this standard.

### Appointment vs walk-in

- **Knowledge test:** three official options — take it **online at any time**; visit a DMV office for **standby**; or **make an appointment**. ([Under-18 permit page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/permit.aspx); [Manual p. 2](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf); appointments and online testing via [DMV2U.Oregon.gov](https://dmv2u.oregon.gov))
- In a DMV office, they **stop giving the knowledge test 1 hour before closing**. ([Under-18 permit page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/permit.aspx))
- After passing the knowledge test online, she **still must go to a DMV office** for the vision test, documents, parent signature, photo, and issuance fee. That visit can be an appointment or standby. ([Under-18 permit page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/permit.aspx); [Online Knowledge Testing FAQ](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_services/online_knowledge_testing.aspx))
- **Drive tests** (later, for the license) are **by appointment only**. ([Manual p. 5](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf); [Under-18 license page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/license.aspx))

### Online knowledge test at home — current rules

Oregon **does** currently offer the official Class C knowledge test online, through DMV2U and vendor Intellectual Technology Inc. (ITI). ([Online Knowledge Testing](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_services/online_knowledge_testing.aspx))

**Equipment and identity**

- Desktop or laptop with keyboard, mouse, and a working **webcam**. **No phones or tablets** (no touchscreen devices). ([Manual p. 3](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf); [Online Knowledge Testing](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_services/online_knowledge_testing.aspx))
- Webcam takes a clear photo at the start and **random photos during the test** to confirm identity. ([Manual p. 3](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf))
- Tester and proctor must provide a **valid SSN** to register. If she has no SSN, or the vendor cannot verify identity, she must test in a DMV office. ([Online Knowledge Testing](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_services/online_knowledge_testing.aspx))
- Closed book: no Driver Manual, notes, or other electronic devices. ([Manual p. 3](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf))

**Proctoring (under 18)**

- A proctor **over 21** must register and **supervise/observe** the test. The proctor does **not** have to be a parent or guardian. ([Manual p. 3](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf); [Online Knowledge Testing FAQ](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_services/online_knowledge_testing.aspx))
- Official limits, which are complementary, not conflicting:
  - A proctor can supervise only **two tests in a 24-hour period**. ([Manual p. 3](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf))
  - A proctor may not have proctored **more than five DMV tests in the past 12 months**. ([Online Knowledge Testing FAQ](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_services/online_knowledge_testing.aspx))
- Testers 18 and older do not need a proctor. ([Online Knowledge Testing FAQ](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_services/online_knowledge_testing.aspx))

**Retakes and waiting periods (online)**

- At most **two** online attempts in any **24-hour period**.
- At most **four** online attempts **total** for that type of knowledge test; after that, she must test at a DMV office.
- Fee is **$7 each attempt**.

([Manual p. 3](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf); [Online Knowledge Testing FAQ](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_services/online_knowledge_testing.aspx))

**In-office retakes:** If she fails in a DMV office, she **may test again the same day**, depending on office testing-resource availability. Official office guidelines I reviewed do **not** state a hard two-per-24-hour cap (that cap is stated for the online test). Cheating fails the test and bars retesting for **90 days**. ([Manual p. 2](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf); [Under-18 permit page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/permit.aspx))

**Warning:** It is a crime (Class C misdemeanor) to let someone else take the test. DMV will suspend driving privileges or the right to apply for **one year**. A person who already has a valid license should not take the official test “for practice”; a fail can affect privileges. Use the official **practice** test instead. ([Online Knowledge Testing](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_services/online_knowledge_testing.aspx))

Technical support: ORK2DOnlineSupport@iti4dmv.com; test emails come from OR_PROD_K2DO@iti4dmv.com. ([Online Knowledge Testing](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_services/online_knowledge_testing.aspx))

### Permit restrictions once issued

A provisional instruction permit allows her to drive only when accompanied by a person who:

- Has a **valid driver license**,
- Is at least **21**, and
- Is **sitting beside** her.

([Under-18 permit page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/permit.aspx); similar language on the [over-18 permit page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/licensepermit.aspx))

For those practice hours to **count** toward the 50- or 100-hour requirement for a provisional license, the supervising driver must also have had their license for at least **3 years**. ([Under-18 permit page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/permit.aspx); [Manual p. 90](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf))

**Cell phones:** She cannot operate a motor vehicle while using a mobile communication device, including talking on a cell phone and texting. **Hands-free accessories are not allowed** for drivers under 18. ([Under-18 permit page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/permit.aspx); [Manual p. 80](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf))

The permit is valid for **2 years** from the issue date. DMV does not send an expiration notice. The card is mailed; the post office will not forward it. ([Under-18 permit page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/permit.aspx))

### Oregon-specific rules that commonly trip people up

All page cites are to the **2026–2027** PDF, <https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf>.

**Speed limits by zone, and the Basic Speed Rule (pp. 19–21)**

Default statutory limits, “whether posted or not,” unless a different speed is posted (p. 19):

- **15 mph** — alleys; narrow residential areas
- **20 mph** — any **business district**; within a **school speed zone**
- **25 mph** — residential districts; public parks; ocean shores if vehicles are permitted
- **55 mph** — all roads and highways not meeting any other definition, unless otherwise posted
- Interstate speed limits “will vary”

The **Basic Rule Law** (pp. 20–21): you must drive at a speed that is **reasonable and cautious for existing conditions**, on all roads at all times. It does **not** allow driving over the speed limit. Driving slower than the limit can still violate the basic rule if the speed is unsafe for conditions. Variable **advisory** speeds are enforced through the basic rule; variable **speed limit** signs are enforced as speed limits (p. 20).

School speed zones are **20 mph**, from the SCHOOL SPEED LIMIT 20 sign to END SCHOOL ZONE or another posted speed sign (p. 49). When the reduced limit applies depends on the sign: flashing yellow light; **7 a.m. to 5 p.m. on a day school is in session** (if unsure whether it is a school day, **slow to 20 mph**); or “when children are present” (children waiting at or in a crosswalk, or a traffic patrol member present) (p. 49).

**Right turn on red / left on red (pp. 15, 39–40)**

After a complete stop on a **steady circular red** (unless a sign or officer says otherwise) (p. 15):

- When entering a **two-way** road, you may turn **right**.
- When entering a **one-way** road, you may turn **right or left** in the direction of the one-way road.

A **steady red arrow** allows the same turns on red as a circular red (p. 15). **No Turn on Red** signs prohibit the turn (p. 9).

More specific turn geometry (pp. 39–40):

- It is **illegal** to make a left turn onto a **two-way** road against a red light (p. 39).
- **Left, one-way to one-way:** may turn on red after stopping and yielding (p. 40).
- **Left or right, two-way to one-way:** may turn on red after stopping and yielding (p. 40).
- **Left, one-way to two-way:** **cannot** turn on red (p. 40).

**Move-over / approaching stopped vehicles (p. 57)**

On a road with **two or more lanes in the same direction**, if you approach **any motor vehicle** stopped with **lights flashing**, you must **change lanes** so you do not drive next to the stopped vehicle. “You may also slow down by at least **5 miles per hour** under the posted speed limit.” Give as much room as safely possible.

The underlying statute is [ORS 811.147](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors811.html). I did not independently extract the full current statutory text in this pass; use the 2026–2027 manual wording for study questions, and re-check the statute if the app quotes the law itself. After an emergency vehicle with lights/siren passes, stay at least **500 feet** behind it (p. 58).

**Cell phone / hands-free (p. 80; teen permit page)**

- **Under 18:** illegal to use a cell phone or other mobile electronic device while driving. The under-18 permit page is explicit: **hands-free accessories are not allowed**. ([Manual p. 80](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf); [Under-18 permit page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/permit.aspx))
- **18 and older:** must use a hands-free accessory that requires only minimal use of a finger to activate or deactivate (p. 80).
- Also illegal: a TV/tablet/video display visible to the driver; a sound system audible **50 feet** from the vehicle (p. 80).

**BAC, zero tolerance under 21, implied consent (pp. 82–83)**

- **Implied Consent:** by driving, you have agreed to take a breath, blood, or urine test when asked by a police officer. Refusal may mean a fine and/or suspension (p. 83).
- **Under 21:** you fail the test if you have a BAC of **any amount**. “Oregon has a **zero tolerance** law.” (p. 83)
- **21 and over:** you fail if BAC is **0.08 percent or more** (p. 83). Oregon law also **presumes** impairment at 0.08%, but you can still be arrested at a lower BAC (p. 82). DUII applies to **any** impairing substance, including prescription and over-the-counter drugs (p. 83).
- **Open container:** illegal to have an open alcohol container in a vehicle on any road; a container is “open” if the seal is broken, even if reclosed. Open containers belong in the trunk (p. 83).

**Following distance (pp. 26–27)**

- A safe following distance is defined as **2–4 seconds**. For speeds greater than **30 mph**, use **4 seconds or more** (p. 26).
- How to measure: when the rear of the vehicle ahead passes a mark, count seconds until you reach the same mark; you are too close if you arrive before finishing at least two seconds (p. 27). Increase the gap on wet roads, when following bikes/motorcycles or large vehicles, with a heavy load, in poor visibility, on hills, in work zones, and while learning (pp. 27–28).
- When stopped behind another vehicle, you should see where its **rear tires meet the road** (p. 27).

**Headlight rules (pp. 76–77)**

- Headlights must be on from **sunset to sunrise**, and any time conditions make it difficult to see people or vehicles **1,000 feet** ahead (p. 76). (This matches the “limited visibility condition” definition used in lighting law; see [ODOT Lights and Swipes](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/safety/pages/lights-and-swipes.aspx) citing ORS 801.325 and 811.515.)
- Dim high beams for **oncoming** traffic within **500 feet**, and when **following** within **350 feet**. When high beams must be dimmed, auxiliary lights such as fog lights must also be off (p. 76).
- Illegal to drive at night or in bad weather with **only parking lights** on (p. 76).
- In fog, dust, or smoke, use **low beams** (p. 77).
- “Increase your visibility to other motorists by turning on your headlights when your windshield wipers are on” (p. 77). This is how the **manual** states it. ODOT Safety’s [Lights and Swipes](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/safety/pages/lights-and-swipes.aspx) page describes a legislatively required awareness campaign (ORS 811.526) “for the law requiring use of headlights when also using your windshield wipers,” but the statutes that page actually quotes are the limited-visibility lighting rules (ORS 811.515 / 801.325), not a separately extracted “wipers-on, lights-on” charging section. **Do not invent a standalone statute number** unless it is re-confirmed in ORS text. For the knowledge test, teach the manual’s headlight and 1,000-foot / sunset-sunrise / dimming numbers, and the manual’s wipers-and-headlights guidance.

**School bus rules (pp. 55–56)**

- Flashing **amber:** bus is about to stop to load or unload; **prepare to stop**.
- Flashing **red** (stop arm may extend): **stop before reaching the bus** and remain stopped until the driver turns the red lights off.
- On a **divided highway** with two roads separated by an **unpaved median or barrier**, stop only if you are on the **same side** as the bus.
- A **painted median or turn lane does not** create two roads; **all lanes must stop**.
- Places-of-worship and worker transport buses with the same flashing lights are treated the **same as a school bus** (p. 56).
- Public transit buses: yield when the driver signals to re-enter a lane and an electric “yield” sign is flashing on the back of the bus (p. 56).

**Roundabouts (pp. 43–44)**

- Traffic moves **counterclockwise**. Yield to traffic **inside and exiting** the roundabout; wait for a gap and merge (p. 43). Stop for pedestrians in the approach crosswalk. Signal **right** to exit. Do not pass a person on a bicycle in the roundabout. In multi-lane roundabouts, choose the correct lane before entering, maintain lane position, and do not pass; it is illegal to pass or drive beside a truck inside the roundabout (p. 44). If emergency vehicles approach, do not enter; if already inside, **do not stop in the circle** — exit, then pull over (p. 44).

**Bike lanes (pp. 39, 50–51)**

- Do **not** drive or park in a bike lane. You may **cross** a bike lane when turning or entering/leaving an alley, driveway, or private road. Do **not** move into a bike lane in preparation for a turn (pp. 39, 50).
- Yield to people bicycling in a bike lane or on a sidewalk before turning across it (pp. 39, 50).
- At speeds greater than **35 mph**, you may pass a person on a bicycle in your lane only if you leave enough distance to prevent contact if they were to fall (p. 50).
- **Bike boxes:** stop **behind** the box, not in it. **No right turn on red** at these intersections. On green, signal and yield to people bicycling on the right (p. 51).

**Pedestrians and crosswalks (pp. 46–48)**

- Stop for pedestrians crossing at **any marked or unmarked** crosswalk. A pedestrian is crossing when any part or extension (cane, wheelchair, bicycle, etc.) moves onto the road (p. 46).
- Remain stopped when the pedestrian is in your lane, in a lane next to yours (including a bike lane), or in the lane you are turning into (p. 46).
- At a **signalized** intersection, before you begin to turn, the pedestrian must be **six feet or more** from the lane you are turning into (p. 47).
- At an intersection **without** a signal, before you begin to turn, the pedestrian must be **past the lane you are turning into, plus the next lane** (p. 47).
- Do not pass a vehicle stopped at a crosswalk (p. 47).
- There is a crosswalk at **every intersection**, even if unmarked. An unmarked crosswalk is at least **six feet** wide (p. 47).
- You need not stop if the pedestrian is in a crosswalk on the **other side of a safety island** (p. 47).
- When crossing a sidewalk (driveway, alley, parking lot), stop before the sidewalk and yield to pedestrians and bicyclists (p. 47).
- White cane or guide dog: remain stopped until the person has crossed the **entire** road, even if you have a green light (p. 48).

**Parking distances (pp. 68, 70–71)**

- Parallel park no more than **12 inches** from the curb (p. 68).
- No parking (among other places) (pp. 70–71):
  - Within **10 feet** of a **fire hydrant**
  - Within **15 feet** of a fire-station driveway on the same side, or **75 feet** on the opposite side
  - Within **20 feet** of a marked or unmarked **crosswalk at an intersection**
  - Within **50 feet** of a traffic signal or sign if your vehicle hides it
  - Within **50 feet** of the nearest rail of a **railroad or light rail** crossing
  - Within **7 1/2 feet** of railroad or light rail tracks when it interferes with the train
  - Also: in a traffic lane; in an intersection; double parking; on a sidewalk, crosswalk, bicycle lane or path; on tracks; on a bridge/overpass/tunnel; in front of a driveway; and where signs or markings prohibit it.

**Other high-yield numbers**

- Signal at least **100 feet** before a turn or lane change when moving in traffic (p. 38).
- Hand-and-arm signals only in daylight when you can see people and vehicles at **1,000 feet**; use signal lights at night or in poor visibility (p. 38).
- Safety belts mandatory for all drivers and passengers in all available seating positions. Child safety seats until **8 years of age or at least 4 feet 9 inches**, and the adult belt fits correctly. Infants rear-facing until **two years of age** (pp. 73–74).
- Disabled-person parking fines begin at **$165** and go up to **$1,000** (p. 70).

**Provisional license restrictions and 50/100 hours** are in section 4 below (manual p. 90 and the teen license pages).

## 4. Graduated licensing path after the permit (short summary)

After the instruction permit, the next credential for a 16- or 17-year-old is a **Provisional Driver License**. ([Under-18 license page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/license.aspx); [ORS 807.065](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors807.html))

**To qualify**

- Be **16 or 17**.
- Have held an instruction permit (Oregon, another state, or D.C.; temporary permits do not count) for at least **6 months**. ([Manual pp. 5, 90](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf); [Under-18 license page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/license.aspx); [Driver Information](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_quick_tips/driver_information.aspx); [ORS 807.065](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors807.html))
- Complete **100 hours** of supervised driving, **or 50 hours** if she has taken and passed an **ODOT-approved driver education** course and DMV receives proof of completion. Supervised hours count only if the supervisor is at least **21** and has had valid driving privileges for at least **3 years**. ([Manual p. 90](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf); [Under-18 license page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/license.aspx); [Parent guide / practice log page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_parent_guide_to_teen_driving/11_rules_for_graduated_licensing.aspx); [ORS 807.065](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors807.html) — the statute frames this as 50 supervised hours plus driver ed, or 50 + another 50 without driver ed.)
- Driver education is **optional**. It is not required to get the permit or the license. Completing an ODOT-approved course within the past **two years** can also **waive the drive test**. If the course is passed on or after October 14, 2024, the provider notifies DMV electronically. ([Under-18 license page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/license.aspx); [Manual p. 3](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf))
- Parent/guardian signature and school-enrollment certification still apply. Pass a vision test. Pay the license fee. Drive test is $45 at DMV unless waived; a failed drive test requires waiting until the **next business day**. ([Under-18 license page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/license.aspx))

**Restrictions after the provisional license is issued** ([Under-18 license page](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/teen/pages/license.aspx); [Parent guide](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_parent_guide_to_teen_driving/11_rules_for_graduated_licensing.aspx))

- Until **18:** still no mobile-device use, including hands-free.
- **First 6 months:** no passenger under **20** who is not immediate family; no driving **midnight–5:00 a.m.** except listed exceptions.
- **Second 6 months:** no **more than three** passengers under 20 who are not immediate family; same midnight–5:00 a.m. rule.
- Night exceptions: between home and work; between home and a school event with no other transportation; driving for employment; or accompanied by a licensed driver at least **25**.
- Passenger restrictions do not apply while driving with a certified instructor, or with a parent, stepparent, or legal guardian who has valid privileges.
- Passenger and night restrictions end after **one year** or at **age 18**, whichever comes first.

## 5. Content licensing

### Oregon Driver Manual and its sign images

- The current PDF identifies ODOT DMV as publisher and carries **Form 735-37 (1-26)©** and STK #300011 on the back cover — a **copyright symbol** is present. I found **no** public-domain dedication, Creative Commons license, or “you may freely reproduce this manual” notice in the 2026–2027 PDF. ([Manual back cover](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/37.pdf))
- ODOT’s own copyright guidance says works created since 1989 are protected unless stated otherwise, and that **“State and local public agencies have the option of using copyright for their publications.”** U.S. **federal** government publications are generally public domain; **Oregon state** publications are not automatically public domain. ([ODOT Library, “Copyright: Navigating the Muddy Waters”](https://www.oregon.gov/ODOT/about/documents/ODOT-Library-Copyright.pdf))
- **Conclusion:** Treat the Oregon Driver Manual’s **text, layout, photos, and any unique artwork in the PDF as copyrighted** unless ODOT/DMV later grants written permission. I could **not** confirm that the manual is public domain or freely reusable.

### Can practice questions be written based on it?

- **Yes, original questions based on the facts** are the conservative path. U.S. copyright does not protect **facts, ideas, or methods**, only the particular expression. The same ODOT guidance states that copyright “does not protect facts, ideas, systems, or methods of operation, although it may protect the way these things are expressed,” citing the U.S. Copyright Office. ([ODOT Library copyright guide](https://www.oregon.gov/ODOT/about/documents/ODOT-Library-Copyright.pdf); [U.S. Copyright Office FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-general.html))
- Write **original** questions and explanations in your own words, citing the official manual (page/section + URL) as the source of the rule. Do **not** copy substantial passages of manual text, and do **not** scrape or reproduce question banks from commercial apps (those are separately copyrighted).
- Official DMV [practice-test questions](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/practice_test.aspx) are published by the state on a public webpage. Reusing those exact 20 items still deserves a cautious approach (no license is stated on that page). Safer: use them as a quality check, and write a larger original bank aligned to the same facts.
- Before shipping an app that embeds manual screenshots, official sign scans from the PDF, or long quoted passages, **ask ODOT/DMV for written permission**. Contact starting point: Oregon DMV, 1905 Lana Ave NE, Salem, OR 97314, 503-945-5000, or the ODOT Library contact listed in the copyright guide.

### MUTCD / federal sign images — public domain

FHWA’s MUTCD states: **“Any traffic control device design or application provision contained in this Manual shall be considered to be in the public domain. Traffic control devices contained in this Manual shall not be protected by a patent, trademark, or copyright, except for the Interstate Shield and any items owned by FHWA.”** ([MUTCD 2009 Edition, Introduction, para. 04](https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009r1r2/intro/intro.htm)) Pictographs embedded in devices are treated separately in the following paragraph.

**Free official and public-domain sign artwork**

- FHWA **Standard Highway Signs** (current 11th Edition / 2024 SHS), including phased PDF, EPS, and **SVG** releases: <https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/kno-shs_2024.htm> and release status <https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/kno-shs_2024-release-status/>
- Wikimedia Commons MUTCD sign diagrams, typically tagged [Template:PD-USGov-MUTCD](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Template:PD-USGov-MUTCD). Examples: [MUTCD R1-1 (Stop)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:MUTCD_R1-1.svg), [MUTCD R1-2 (Yield)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:MUTCD_R1-2.svg). Licensing notes on shields: <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Licensing/U.S._road_shields>
- Prefer FHWA SVGs or Commons files with a clear PD-USGov-MUTCD tag. Do **not** use the Interstate Shield or other FHWA-owned marks without checking. Do **not** rip sign bitmaps out of the Oregon PDF if a federal MUTCD equivalent exists.

Oregon-specific signs or ODOT photographs in the manual may still be state-copyrighted even if the underlying MUTCD design is public domain. When in doubt, redraw from FHWA artwork.

## 7. Study science that matters for a teen

Two techniques have the strongest evidence for long-term retention, and they map cleanly onto a permit app: **practice testing (retrieval practice)** and **spaced (distributed) practice**. Combining them — short, test-like quizzes restudied over days, with extra repetitions on missed items — is more effective than rereading the manual the night before.

**Practice testing / retrieval practice.** Taking a test is not just assessment; it strengthens memory more than restudying the same material. Roediger and Karpicke (2006) showed that testing produces better long-term retention than repeated study, even when the test is given without further study of the text. ([“Test-Enhanced Learning,” *Psychological Science*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x))

**Spaced practice plus practice testing, as high-utility techniques.** Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan, and Willingham (2013) reviewed common study techniques and rated **practice testing** and **distributed practice** as the two highest-utility methods for students. Cramming can feel effective in the short term but loses to spacing for retention. Feedback after a practice item helps correct errors. ([“Improving Students’ Learning With Effective Learning Techniques,” *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100612453266))

**App implications (not new research — just applying those papers):**

- A mode that **mirrors the real test** (35 items, 28 to pass, mixed signs and rules, closed-book, no instant peek at the manual) trains the actual performance.
- After each session, tag missed topics (school-zone speed, left on red, move-over, parking distances, BAC, etc.) and **re-queue those items after expanding intervals**.
- Interleave signs and rules rather than blocking all signs, then all speeds.
- Explain the correct answer with a manual page cite, then require retrieval again later — do not let “I just reread it” count as mastery.
